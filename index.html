Who you'd be working with

I started by writing code. I spent the last 13 years on the executive team, deciding what it should do.

Thirty-eight years in technology have taught me that every major shift forces you to relearn the playbook—and reinventing myself through each one has been the best part of the job. I built software before office PCs were networked, watched Mosaic (the first web browser) and the internet rewrite how businesses reach customers, kept a bank steady through the 2008 financial crisis, moved core systems to the cloud and onto mobile, and shifted an entire workforce remote overnight in 2020 with zero downtime. Along the way, I've been through six mergers and acquisitions from both sides of the table—as the buyer and as the company being bought—and led the technology side as an executive. Now I'm helping leadership teams navigate the biggest shift yet: AI.

Every one of those waves raised the same questions your team is asking today: What should we do? What will it really cost? Who will make it work?

How I lead

Clarity, trust, and room to run.

Great teams don't need micromanagement. I believe in treating people with genuine respect, giving them straight answers, and making sure everyone understands the why behind what we're building. From there, I empower people to take real ownership and calculated risks, keeping smart guardrails in place so they can stretch, learn fast, and recover quickly when things don't go as planned.

I also coach. I share my reasoning, let my leaders make the call, and talk it through with them afterward. In a fractional role, that matters most: when I step away, the judgment stays with your team.
